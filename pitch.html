The pitch · Masters' Union · Term 1 · UG TBM · Section A

Luxury never upgrades.

Rolex doesn't sell time. We don't sell a phone. We sell the one you keep.

01 · Problem

The most expensive things you own are the ones you keep. Your phone is the one you throw away.

The treadmill

Flagships are replaced every 2–3 years. Buyers who spend on craftsmanship everywhere else get a disposable slab with their data inside.

No status, no soul

Every flagship looks the same. A ₹2L iPhone and a ₹80k one are indistinguishable on a table.

The shape

A rectangle. The human hand has gripped curved stones since the stone age.

02 · Solution

A phone engineered like a timepiece. Built once. Kept for life.

Made to last

Sapphire crystal, steel and titanium body, hand finishing, crown control.

Made for the hand

A flat screen on a domed, palm-filling back. Ergonomics from human anatomy, not a spec sheet.

Made to stay

The design never changes. We service it for life instead of selling an upgrade.

Fit: who buys it

Existing luxury watch and goods buyers. They already pay for craftsmanship and exclusivity over specs. India's ultra-rich population is projected to grow about 50% by 2028, and premium phones are now over a fifth of shipments.

03 · Our USP

It doesn't change. That is the luxury.

06 · Shark questions

Ask us anything.

The tech inside is the same as any flagship. Why pay this much?

You're buying craftsmanship, exclusivity and a promise. A mechanical watch and a quartz one tell the same time; one is kept for generations.

Apple or Samsung could crush you.

They chase volume, we chase scarcity. Apple earns 28% of India's smartphone value from 9% of units. We play at the top of that, with 1,000 units.

What are your unit economics?

₹4,50,000 price, about ₹1,60,000 to build and finish, so roughly ₹2,90,000 gross profit per phone before service revenue.

Vertu failed. Why won't you?

Vertu sold jewellery wrapped round a stale phone. We use current flagship internals, so the phone is never behind, and we add lifetime service so the object keeps its value.

Lifetime servicing sounds like a liability.

It's a paid plan with modular battery and glass, backed by a reserve from every sale. It's also the reason the buyer never leaves.

Isn't artificial scarcity a gimmick?

Scarcity is the product. A numbered edition has a serial, a registry and a resale price. That's how the watch world works.
